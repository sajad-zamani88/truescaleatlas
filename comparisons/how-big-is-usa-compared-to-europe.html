How Big Is the USA Compared to Europe?

The United States covers 3,796,742 sq mi (9.83 million km²). Europe covers about 3,930,000 sq mi (10.18 million km²). Europe is only 3.5% larger — a gap about the size of Germany. But the USA is 1.58× larger than Europe excluding Russia, and 2.32× larger than the European Union.

Most Americans assume Europe dwarfs the United States. It doesn't. The two are nearly identical in area — and the reason they look so different on a world map is the Mercator projection, a 1569 navigation chart still powering most web maps today.

USA vs Europe: the numbers

Why the map lies to you

Mercator stretches area by sec²(latitude). At the U.S. mean latitude of 39°N that is 1.66×. At Europe's mean latitude of 48°N it is 2.23×. Divide them and you get 1.35 — Europe looks about 35% bigger than it should relative to America, purely because it sits farther north

Overlay the U.S. on Europe at true scale and the two nearly coincide. Open the interactive map, drag America across the Atlantic, then slide it toward the equator and watch the inflation disappear.

Key takeaways

  • The USA and Europe are within 3.5% of each other in total area.

    The gap is about 133,000 sq mi — roughly the size of Germany.

    Mercator inflates Europe ~35% more than the USA because Europe sits ~9° farther north.

    Europe is twice as densely populated: 190 vs 96 people per sq mi.

Which Europe do you mean?

This is where most arguments go wrong. Include European Russia and Europe edges ahead. Remove it and the USA is 1.58× larger. Compare against the 27-member European Union and the USA is 2.32× larger. Same question, three correct answers  and Texas alone is bigger than France.

Is the USA bigger than Europe?
Not quite. Europe (10.18M km²) is about 3.5% larger than the USA (9.83M km²). But the USA is larger than Europe without Russia, and 2.32× larger than the EU.
Is the USA bigger than the European Union?
Yes — about 2.32 times larger. The EU-27 covers roughly 4.23 million km² versus America's 9.83 million.
Why does Europe look so much bigger on a world map?
Mercator inflates area at high latitudes. Europe's mean latitude (~48°N) is exaggerated 2.23× versus 1.66× for the USA (~39°N).
Which has more people, the USA or Europe?
Europe. About 745 million versus 342 million — roughly 2.2× — on nearly the same land area.
