Method & accuracy

Transparent geometry, honest numbers.

How true-size mode works

Each outline is converted to unit-sphere vectors, rotated from its original spherical centroid to the target latitude and longitude, optionally spun around the target axis, then projected with Mercator. This preserves spherical area exactly for the given boundary.

area ≈ R² · Σ spherical_ring_excess   |   R = 6371.0088 km
rotation: Rodrigues(v, axis, θ) → Mercator(lon, lat)

Data & limitations

Boundaries: Natural Earth 1:110m public-domain data via world-atlas. Areas are computed from these generalized outlines, so they are excellent for visual education but are not official national statistics. Coastlines and small islands are simplified.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Why does Greenland look as large as Africa on many maps?

Mercator stretches areas near the poles. Greenland appears huge, but Africa is about 14 times larger in real area.

How does TrueScale Atlas keep country sizes accurate?

Country outlines are rotated on a sphere before projection, so their spherical area stays constant while their Mercator appearance changes with latitude.

Are the area numbers official statistics?

No. They are computed from Natural Earth 1:110m boundaries and are intended for education and visual comparison, not legal or survey use.

Do I need an API key or backend?

No. Libraries and data load from public CDNs, and all interaction, saving, and exporting happens in your browser.

Can I upload the exported file to my host?

Yes. Use “HTML page” to download a standalone snapshot, or host this single index.html file on any static host such as GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, or shared hosting.
